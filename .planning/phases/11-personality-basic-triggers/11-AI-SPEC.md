## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Multi-Agent Stateful Workflow with Proactive Triggers

**Description:**
Phase 11 extends the existing LangGraph graph to wire two specialized AI bot roles — a Coach (FacilitationAgentNode) and an Analyst/Fact-Checker (AnalyticsAgentNode) — into a synchronous, multi-user collaborative workspace. The system classifies incoming triggers (silence, analysis requests) and routes them to the correct node via a conditional START edge; the ArgGraphBuilderNode maintains a live argument graph in LangGraph thread state and injects it as context into each Analyst invocation. A minimal interim silence-scan loop fires a content-aware Coach question into the conversation after N seconds of no human activity. "Good" means the Coach always ends with a question (never a statement), the Analyst always cites a prior message by name, routing never silently defaults, and the silence trigger respects its cooldown window.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
<!-- The 3-5 behaviors that absolutely cannot go wrong in this system -->
1. **Silent routing default** — the conditional START edge silently routes to the wrong node (FacilitationAgentNode vs AnalyticsAgentNode) without logging an error, violating ROADMAP success criterion 2
2. **Role behavioral contract violation** — a Coach output ends with a statement instead of a question, or an Analyst output cites no specific prior message, breaking the core user value proposition
3. **Silence trigger re-fires during cooldown** — the trigger fires multiple Coach messages during a single cooldown window, spamming the conversation and breaking ROADMAP success criterion 3
4. **argGraph state corruption** — ArgGraphBuilderNode writes to the wrong thread_id or reducer corrupts accumulated state across invocations, making Analyst prompts stale or incoherent
5. **Personality voice overrides Role rules** — Personality styling bleeds into behavioral output (e.g., a casual personality tone causes the Coach to stop asking questions), violating the D-03 precedence rule
6. **Bots working on a frozen session** - if the session is frozen then no AI activity should happen, if AI keeps participating in a frozen session then it will waste tokens etc.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.
> Research date: 2026-07-10. Sources: CHI 2025 deliberation studies, arxiv facilitation research, Socratic AI practitioner literature.

**Industry Vertical:** Collaborative intelligence / facilitated deliberation / group decision-making technology

**User Population:** Small groups (3–15 participants) engaged in synchronous structured debate and idea exploration; session creators managing workspace configuration and bot activation; Spanish-speaking, informal register (tú)

**Stakes Level:** Medium

**Output Consequence:** Coach questions land in a live group chat and are seen by all participants in real time. A poorly formed Coach question can derail the group's reasoning trajectory, cause premature closure of a productive thread, or — if generic/decontextualized — signal that the AI is not tracking the actual conversation, eroding trust in the entire facilitation system. Analyst citations that name the wrong speaker or misrepresent a claim can create interpersonal friction within the group and undermine the credibility of the argument map that the group may use to make decisions.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

A trained group facilitator or deliberation researcher would evaluate this system on the following dimensions.

---

**Dimension: Genuine inquiry vs. rhetorical closure**
Good (facilitator accepts): Coach output ends with an open question that invites multiple possible answers and deepens the current thread — e.g., "Miguel mencionó la calidad de la evidencia — ¿qué criterios usarían para evaluarla?"
Bad (facilitator flags): Coach output ends with a leading question that presupposes an answer, or a statement dressed as a question — e.g., "¿No creen que ya tienen suficiente evidencia?" (implies they should stop) or "Es claro que necesitan más datos." (no question at all)
Stakes: Critical
Source: Socratic dialogue practice; CHI 2025 deliberative AI research identifies "premature consensus" and "false closure" as the primary failure mode of AI facilitators (arxiv.org/html/2503.07690v1); REQUIREMENTS.md PERSONA-01

---

**Dimension: Content-awareness of the silence-break question**
Good (facilitator accepts): When silence trigger fires, the Coach references specific content from the conversation — a named claim, a speaker's position, an unresolved tension visible in the argGraph — e.g., "Hace un momento Laura propuso que el costo era el factor decisivo, pero Ana cuestionó eso — ¿quieren explorar esa tensión?"
Bad (facilitator flags): Coach fires a generic prompt that could apply to any conversation — e.g., "¿Alguien quiere agregar algo?" or "¿Cómo se sienten con el debate hasta ahora?" — with no reference to actual conversation content
Stakes: High
Source: D-13 (CONTEXT.md) — explicit product decision; deliberation research shows decontextualized AI prompts cause participant disengagement (arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14097); the silence trigger is precisely where generic outputs are most likely to occur

---

**Dimension: Citation specificity in Analyst outputs**
Good (facilitator accepts): Analyst names the speaker and paraphrases the specific claim being analyzed — e.g., "Cuando Carlos dijo que 'el modelo funciona en el 90% de los casos', no quedó claro cuál fue la muestra de prueba — ¿pueden ampliar eso?"
Bad (facilitator flags): Analyst makes a general observation about the conversation without anchoring to a specific prior message — e.g., "Se han hecho varias afirmaciones sobre la efectividad del modelo que necesitan verificación." No speaker named, no specific claim referenced.
Stakes: Critical
Source: REQUIREMENTS.md PERSONA-02; practitioner standard — unsourced analytical claims in facilitated dialogue create confusion about which participant is being questioned and can feel accusatory without attribution

---

**Dimension: Role discipline under Personality styling**
Good (facilitator accepts): Even when the Coach personality has a casual or playful voice, every single output still ends with a question mark. The Analyst, even with a formal academic voice, still anchors every output to a named speaker.
Bad (facilitator flags): A Personality voice with a casual, conversational tone causes the Coach to emit a concluding statement ("¡Excelente punto!") without a follow-up question. The Analyst, in a warm/empathetic Personality mode, hedges so much it forgets to name a speaker.
Stakes: Critical
Source: D-03 precedence rule (CONTEXT.md) — locked architectural decision; Springer Nature research on Socratic AI identifies "value-laden neutrality" and personality voice drift as a top-tier failure mode (link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-025-00567-8)

---

**Dimension: Non-intrusiveness of proactive interventions**
Good (facilitator accepts): Over a 30-minute session, the Coach fires the silence trigger at most 2–3 times, each time with a question that moves the conversation forward. Participants experience it as a natural prompt, not a disruption.
Bad (facilitator flags): Multiple Coach messages appear in rapid succession, or the Coach fires during active typing, or a second Coach question lands before anyone has responded to the first. Participants start ignoring Coach messages entirely (trust collapse signal).
Stakes: High
Source: REQUIREMENTS.md BOT-02 (arbitration lock), BOT-03 (two-signal silence gate), TRIGGER-01 (once per cooldown window); deliberation research on "AI prominence" showing that overly active AI facilitators suppress human participation (arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14407)

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

**1. Premature closure / false consensus**
The Coach, drawing on a partial argGraph, asks a synthesis question ("¿Están de acuerdo entonces en que X es la solución?") before the group has actually converged, manufacturing apparent consensus. Participants who disagree stay silent rather than contradicting what sounds like a group summary. This is the most-cited failure mode of AI-facilitated deliberation in CHI 2025 research.

**2. Role bleed — Analyst becomes a debater**
The Analyst, instead of citing and questioning a prior claim, adds new argumentation of its own — effectively becoming a participant in the debate rather than an observer of it. In practice: "Carlos dijo que el modelo tiene 90% de precisión. En realidad, los estudios recientes muestran que 90% es insuficiente para este caso." The second sentence is a counter-assertion, not a facilitation move. This violates PERSONA-02 (uncertainty framing only) and has been observed in production LLM facilitation deployments.

**3. Generic silence-break (decontextualized prompt)**
The silence trigger fires with a question that has no connection to actual conversation content. Participants correctly perceive the Coach as not tracking the discussion. After 2–3 such incidents, the Coach bot loses credibility and participants stop engaging with its questions. This is distinct from hallucination — the output is grammatically correct but socially useless.

**4. Cooldown bypass / bot flooding**
If the cooldown enforcement logic is bypassed (e.g., due to the setInterval callback not being awaited), the Coach fires multiple times in a short window. In a Spanish-language group debate context, rapid-fire AI interventions are perceived as the AI trying to control the conversation rather than facilitate it, causing participants to mentally dismiss the bot. This is irreversible within a session once it happens.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No domain-specific regulation governs AI-facilitated group deliberation in this deployment context (private collaborative workspace tool, BYOK model, no healthcare/financial/legal content domains in v1 scope).

Standard data handling considerations apply: session transcripts contain participant names, conversation content, and potentially sensitive organizational information. Supabase RLS and per-session data isolation are the relevant controls — already in scope as a platform constraint, not a new compliance requirement.

Note: If future Blueprint domains extend into regulated content areas (e.g., a healthcare decision Blueprint, a financial planning Blueprint), HIPAA or FCA guidance on AI-assisted decision support would become relevant. Flag this at the Blueprint expansion phase.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** LangGraph (@langchain/langgraph)

**Version:** 1.4.7 (already installed; @langchain/langgraph-checkpoint-postgres 1.0.4)

**Rationale:**
This phase extends an existing LangGraph graph — not a greenfield choice. The Phase 10 infrastructure (GraphStateAnnotation with argGraph/triggerMetadata reducers, PostgresSaver checkpointer, dual thread_id separation, conditional edges via routeAfterOrchestrator) is all LangGraph-native and load-bearing. Phase 11's deliverables (conditional START edge, ArgGraphBuilderNode state population, interim setInterval silence-scan) map directly onto LangGraph's addConditionalEdges, StateAnnotation reducers, and node registration primitives. Langfuse tracing (@langfuse/langchain 5.9.1) is already instrumented and will automatically cover new nodes.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| CrewAI | Python-only; entire codebase is TypeScript; would require full stack rewrite |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Wrong model vendor (Claude-committed per CLAUDE.md); no persistent state management |
| LangChain (without LangGraph) | Linear chains insufficient for stateful multi-agent routing; Phase 10 already committed to LangGraph graph topology |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — Anthropic/Claude locked per CLAUDE.md v1 constraint; LangGraph TypeScript locked by existing Phase 10 infrastructure. Both trade-offs are documented and accepted.

### Entry Point Pattern

This is the Phase 11 conditional START edge pattern — extending the existing `createGraph()` factory in `apps/api/src/graph/graph.ts`.

```typescript
import { StateGraph, START, END, MemorySaver } from '@langchain/langgraph'
import type { BaseCheckpointSaver } from '@langchain/langgraph'
import { GraphStateAnnotation } from './state'
import type { GraphState } from './state'
import { orchestratorNode } from './nodes/orchestrator'
import { agentNode } from './nodes/agent'
import { mutationGateNode } from './nodes/mutation-gate'
import { driftReplyNode } from './nodes/drift-reply'
import { argGraphBuilderNode } from './nodes/arg-graph-builder'
import { facilitationAgentNode } from './nodes/facilitation-agent'
import { analyticsAgentNode } from './nodes/analytics-agent'

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Existing post-orchestrator router (unchanged from Phase 10)
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
export function routeAfterOrchestrator(state: GraphState): 'agent' | 'driftReply' | 'end' {
  if (state.guardrailResult === 'DOMAIN_DRIFT') {
    return state.driftAction === 'replied' ? 'driftReply' : 'end'
  }
  return 'agent'
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Phase 11: START-edge router
// Returns a string key that maps to a node name in the pathsMap below.
// CRITICAL: every possible return value MUST appear in the pathsMap —
// LangGraph silently drops the invocation if a key is missing.
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
export function routeFromStart(
  state: GraphState,
): 'facilitation' | 'analysis' | 'orchestrator' {
  const triggerType = (state.triggerMetadata as Record<string, unknown>)?.trigger_type

  if (triggerType === 'silence_gate') {
    console.info('[graph] START → facilitation (silence_gate trigger)')
    return 'facilitation'
  }
  if (triggerType === 'analysis_request') {
    console.info('[graph] START → analysis (analysis_request trigger)')
    return 'analysis'
  }
  // Default: human message — no trigger_type set → existing orchestrator pipeline
  console.info('[graph] START → orchestrator (human message)')
  return 'orchestrator'
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// createGraph factory — Phase 11 extends Phase 10 graph
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
export function createGraph(checkpointer?: BaseCheckpointSaver) {
  const graph = new StateGraph(GraphStateAnnotation)
    // Existing nodes (unchanged)
    .addNode('orchestrator', orchestratorNode)
    .addNode('agent', agentNode)
    .addNode('mutationGate', mutationGateNode)
    .addNode('driftReply', driftReplyNode)
    // Phase 11 new nodes
    .addNode('argGraphBuilder', argGraphBuilderNode)
    .addNode('facilitation', facilitationAgentNode)
    .addNode('analysis', analyticsAgentNode)

    // Phase 11: conditional START edge replaces the fixed START → orchestrator edge.
    // Remove .addEdge(START, 'orchestrator') — having both causes the fixed edge to win.
    .addConditionalEdges(START, routeFromStart, {
      facilitation: 'facilitation',
      analysis: 'argGraphBuilder',  // ArgGraphBuilderNode runs first, populates argGraph
      orchestrator: 'orchestrator',
    })

    // Existing post-orchestrator edges (unchanged)
    .addConditionalEdges('orchestrator', routeAfterOrchestrator, {
      agent: 'agent',
      driftReply: 'driftReply',
      end: END,
    })
    .addEdge('agent', 'mutationGate')
    .addEdge('mutationGate', END)
    .addEdge('driftReply', END)

    // Phase 11 new edges
    .addEdge('argGraphBuilder', 'analysis')
    .addEdge('facilitation', END)
    .addEdge('analysis', END)

  return graph.compile({ checkpointer: checkpointer ?? new MemorySaver() })
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| `Annotation.Root` | Defines the typed state schema with per-field reducers. `Annotation<T>()` (no-arg) is a LastValue channel but does NOT support a `default` value. For fields that must be readable before first write, use `reducer: (_, v) => v` with an explicit `default` — see the `argGraph` and `triggerMetadata` fields in `state.ts`. | Defining `GraphStateAnnotation`. Already complete in Phase 10 — do not re-declare existing fields. |
| `addConditionalEdges(START, fn, pathsMap)` | Routes the very first graph edge based on runtime trigger type. `START` is the LangGraph pseudo-node for graph entry. The `pathsMap` must exhaustively list every string the router function can return — missing keys cause silent invocation drops. | Phase 11 START edge that routes between `facilitation`, `analysis`, and `orchestrator` paths. |
| `config.configurable` | Runtime bag passed to every node via the second argument. Used to inject Blueprint, provider key, test adapters, streamWriter, and Personality record — without baking them into checkpointed state. | Every node — read `config?.configurable?.blueprint`, `?.agentAdapter`, `?.facilityPersonality`, etc. Existing pattern in `agent.ts` and `orchestrator.ts`. |
| `PostgresSaver` | Postgres-backed checkpointer that snapshots the full `GraphState` after every node completes. Thread isolation is via `thread_id` in `configurable`. `thread_id` must be under 255 characters. | Production invocations — already wired via `createGraph(checkpointer)`. Phase 11 nodes are automatically checkpointed. Bot invocations use `bot-${branchId}` thread, never the human thread. |
| `StateGraph.compile({ checkpointer })` | Produces the runnable `CompiledStateGraph`. Pass `MemorySaver` for unit tests, `PostgresSaver` for production and integration tests. Never call `getCheckpointer()` inside graph construction — unit tests cannot control it. | `createGraph()` factory — the only call site. |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **`addConditionalEdges` pathsMap incomplete — silent invocation drop.** If `routeFromStart` can return `'facilitation'` but the pathsMap only contains `{ analysis, orchestrator }`, LangGraph does not throw — it silently drops the entire graph invocation. Always enumerate every possible return string in the map, even the error/default case. This is the root cause of Critical Failure Mode 1 (silent routing default).

2. **Fixed `START` edge conflicts with conditional `START` edge — fixed edge wins silently.** The Phase 10 `graph.ts` has `.addEdge(START, 'orchestrator')`. Phase 11 must remove this line before adding `.addConditionalEdges(START, routeFromStart, ...)`. If both are present, compilation does not error but the fixed edge takes precedence and the router function is never called.

3. **`Annotation<T>()` (no-arg) has no `default` — returns `undefined` on first invocation.** Using `Annotation<string>()` leaves the field as `undefined` until some node writes to it. For `triggerMetadata`, which the silence-scan loop must populate as the initial invocation input, use the explicit `reducer: (_, v) => v, default: () => ({})` form — already done in `state.ts`. Do not add new state fields with the no-arg form if any node reads them before first write.

4. **Proactive bot invocation uses the wrong `thread_id` — checkpoint bleeding.** When the silence-scan `setInterval` calls `graph.invoke(input, config)`, `config.configurable.thread_id` must be the bot thread id (`bot-${branchId}`), not the human thread id. Mixing thread ids causes the bot's state snapshot to overwrite human conversation checkpoints or vice versa, corrupting `state.messages` and `state.argGraph` for human sessions.

5. **Throwing from a node kills the graph run — no automatic catch.** LangGraph TypeScript does not wrap node-thrown errors — they propagate to the `graph.invoke()` caller. All nodes must follow the fail-silent / fail-open convention established in `agent.ts` and `orchestrator.ts`: wrap the entire body in `try/catch`, log with `console.error('[nodename] ...')`, and return `{}` (or a safe partial state) on error. Never `throw` from a node function.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

All model selection goes through `TASK_MODELS` in `apps/api/src/lib/model-config.ts` — never hardcode a model string in a node. Phase 11 requires a `facilitation` tier in the registry (add it if absent):

- `FacilitationAgentNode` (Coach): `TASK_MODELS[providerName].facilitation` — must resolve to a lightweight, fast model (e.g. `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`). Coach outputs are short facilitative questions; latency matters more than reasoning depth.
- `AnalyticsAgentNode` (Analyst): `TASK_MODELS[providerName].analysis` — already defined. The Analyst needs stronger reasoning to cite prior messages accurately.
- `ArgGraphBuilderNode`: `TASK_MODELS[providerName].classification` — argument graph extraction is structured JSON extraction, not generation. Reuse the cheapest tier.

Key `maxTokens` values — always set explicitly, never leave unset in production:

| Node | `maxTokens` | Rationale |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| FacilitationAgentNode | 256 | Coach questions are 1-3 sentences — hard cap prevents runaway generation |
| AnalyticsAgentNode | 512 | Citation + brief analysis — needs more room than Coach |
| ArgGraphBuilderNode | 256 | Structured JSON extraction — output is compact |
| OrchestratorNode | 32 | Classification label only (already enforced) |

Temperature: do not set explicitly — use the model default. If deterministic citation is required for the Analyst, `temperature: 0` is appropriate, but verify against the `AdapterStreamOptions` type before adding the parameter.

**Core Pattern:**

The Role/Personality composition pattern. Every new node follows the four-step skeleton established in `agent.ts` and `orchestrator.ts`: (1) read all runtime deps from `config.configurable`; (2) build system prompt with Role rules structurally dominant (D-03); (3) stream via adapter; (4) return partial state — no direct DB writes from node body.

```typescript
// facilitation-agent.ts — FacilitationAgentNode (Coach role)
// Role contract: every output MUST end with a question mark. No statements. No conclusions.
// Personality: injected via config.configurable.facilityPersonality
// Never imports @anthropic-ai/sdk directly — all LLM access via createAdapter()

import type { Blueprint, ProviderName } from '@panelito/types'
import type { Personality } from '@panelito/types'   // [NEW] Phase 11
import { createAdapter } from '../../lib/adapter-factory'
import { TASK_MODELS } from '../../lib/model-config'
import type { GraphState } from '../state'

// eslint-disable-next-line @typescript-eslint/no-explicit-any
export async function facilitationAgentNode(state: GraphState, config?: any): Promise<Partial<GraphState>> {
  // Step 1: Read runtime deps from config.configurable (test-seam pattern from agent.ts)
  const blueprint = config?.configurable?.blueprint as Blueprint | undefined
  const providerName = config?.configurable?.providerName as ProviderName | undefined
  const plaintextKey = config?.configurable?.plaintextKey as string | undefined
  const personality = config?.configurable?.facilityPersonality as Personality | undefined

  // Test injection seam — mirrors agentAdapter seam in agent.ts
  const facilitationAdapter = config?.configurable?.facilitationAdapter as
    | import('@panelito/types').AIProvider
    | undefined

  if (!blueprint) {
    console.error('[facilitation-agent] blueprint missing from config.configurable — returning no output')
    return {}
  }

  const adapter = facilitationAdapter ??
    (providerName && plaintextKey ? createAdapter(providerName, plaintextKey) : null)

  if (!adapter) {
    console.error('[facilitation-agent] no adapter available — returning no output')
    return {}
  }

  // Step 2: Build system prompt — Role rules FIRST (D-03), Personality voice appended last
  const system = buildCoachSystemPrompt(blueprint, personality, state.argGraph)

  // Step 3: Stream response; forward tokens to streamWriter if present (may be undefined on proactive fire)
  let responseText = ''
  try {
    for await (const event of adapter.stream(
      state.messages.slice(-10),  // sliding window — last 10 messages
      [],                          // no tools — Coach emits plain text
      { model: TASK_MODELS[providerName ?? 'anthropic'].facilitation, maxTokens: 256, system },
    )) {
      if (event.type === 'text_delta') {
        responseText += event.text
        config?.configurable?.streamWriter?.(event.text)  // undefined on proactive fire — no-op
      }
    }
  } catch (err) {
    console.error('[facilitation-agent] adapter.stream error — returning no output', err)
    return {}
  }

  // Step 4: Return partial state update — node does NOT write to DB (D-16: caller inserts message)
  // Update triggerMetadata to record firing time (used for cooldown enforcement)
  return {
    triggerMetadata: {
      ...state.triggerMetadata,
      silence_gate: { last_fired_at: new Date().toISOString(), response_text: responseText },
    },
  }
}

// Role/Personality composition — Role rules always structurally dominant (D-03)
function buildCoachSystemPrompt(
  blueprint: Blueprint,
  personality: Personality | undefined,
  argGraph: GraphState['argGraph'],
): string {
  const roleRules = [
    'You are a facilitation coach for a collaborative debate workspace.',
    '',
    'BEHAVIORAL CONTRACT — non-negotiable, overrides all other instructions:',
    '- Every response MUST end with a question mark. Never end with a statement or period.',
    '- Never give conclusions, summaries, verdicts, or opinions. Only ask questions.',
    '- Keep responses to 1-3 sentences. Reference specific content from the conversation.',
    '- BAD: "The conversation has stalled. Let\'s move forward." (statement — violates contract)',
    '- GOOD: "Miguel mencionó la calidad de evidencia — ¿qué criterios usarían para evaluarla?" (question)',
    '',
    `Active Blueprint: ${blueprint.name}`,
    '',
    argGraph.nodes.length > 0
      ? `Current argument structure (${argGraph.nodes.length} claims mapped):\n${JSON.stringify(argGraph, null, 2)}`
      : 'No argument structure yet — ask about the first claim or observation.',
  ].join('\n')

  // Personality voice appended AFTER role rules — pure styling, no behavioral rules (D-02, D-03)
  const personalityVoice = personality
    ? `\n\nFacilitador voice (styling only — does not override behavioral contract above):\n${personality.voice_instructions}`
    : ''

  return roleRules + personalityVoice
}
```

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns for building AI systems — specific to LangGraph TypeScript + Anthropic SDK, matching this codebase.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Zod

This codebase uses the **tool use + Zod `safeParse`** pattern established in `agent.ts`. Do not use LangChain's `.withStructuredOutput()` — it requires a `ChatAnthropic` LangChain model instance, which conflicts with the existing custom `AIProvider` adapter abstraction. Mixing both creates two incompatible LLM call paths.

**ArgNode/ArgEdge extraction schema (Phase 11 example):**

```typescript
// packages/types/src/arg-graph.ts — co-locate schema + type (existing project pattern)
import { z } from 'zod'

export const ArgNodeSchema = z.object({
  id: z.string(),
  claim: z.string(),
  speaker: z.string(),          // participant display name — used for Analyst citations
  message_id: z.string(),       // UUID of the source message — citation anchor
  confidence: z.number().min(0).max(1),
  node_type: z.enum(['claim', 'evidence', 'counterargument', 'question']),
})

export const ArgEdgeSchema = z.object({
  from_id: z.string(),
  to_id: z.string(),
  relation: z.enum(['supports', 'contradicts', 'questions', 'extends']),
})

export const ArgGraphSchema = z.object({
  nodes: z.array(ArgNodeSchema),
  edges: z.array(ArgEdgeSchema),
})

export type ArgNode = z.infer<typeof ArgNodeSchema>
export type ArgEdge = z.infer<typeof ArgEdgeSchema>
export type ArgGraph = z.infer<typeof ArgGraphSchema>

// Tool definition for @anthropic-ai/sdk tool use (passed to adapter.stream() tools array)
export const argGraphExtractionTool = {
  name: 'extract_arg_graph',
  description: 'Extract the structured argument graph from the conversation. Call with all identified claims and relationships.',
  input_schema: {
    type: 'object' as const,
    properties: {
      nodes: {
        type: 'array',
        items: {
          type: 'object',
          properties: {
            id: { type: 'string' },
            claim: { type: 'string' },
            speaker: { type: 'string' },
            message_id: { type: 'string' },
            confidence: { type: 'number' },
            node_type: { type: 'string', enum: ['claim', 'evidence', 'counterargument', 'question'] },
          },
          required: ['id', 'claim', 'speaker', 'message_id', 'confidence', 'node_type'],
        },
      },
      edges: {
        type: 'array',
        items: {
          type: 'object',
          properties: {
            from_id: { type: 'string' },
            to_id: { type: 'string' },
            relation: { type: 'string', enum: ['supports', 'contradicts', 'questions', 'extends'] },
          },
          required: ['from_id', 'to_id', 'relation'],
        },
      },
    },
    required: ['nodes', 'edges'],
  },
} as const
```

**Retry logic in ArgGraphBuilderNode (max 2 retries on Zod validation failure):**

```typescript
const MAX_RETRIES = 2

async function extractArgGraph(
  adapter: AIProvider,
  messages: ProviderMessage[],
  system: string,
  model: string,
  attempt = 0,
): Promise<ArgGraph | null> {
  let rawInput: unknown = null

  try {
    for await (const event of adapter.stream(messages, [argGraphExtractionTool], {
      model,
      maxTokens: 256,
      system,
    })) {
      if (event.type === 'tool_use' && event.name === 'extract_arg_graph') {
        rawInput = event.input
        break
      }
    }
  } catch (err) {
    console.error('[arg-graph-builder] stream error on attempt', attempt, err)
    return null
  }

  if (rawInput === null) {
    // Model produced no tool call — not a schema failure, log separately
    console.warn('[arg-graph-builder] no tool_use event received on attempt', attempt)
    return null
  }

  const parsed = ArgGraphSchema.safeParse(rawInput)
  if (parsed.success) {
    return parsed.data
  }

  // Zod validation failed — log error details, retry with correction prompt
  console.error('[arg-graph-builder] ArgGraphSchema.safeParse failed', {
    attempt,
    errors: parsed.error.flatten(),
  })

  if (attempt < MAX_RETRIES) {
    const retryMessages: ProviderMessage[] = [
      ...messages,
      {
        role: 'user',
        content:
          `Your previous extract_arg_graph call failed validation: ` +
          `${JSON.stringify(parsed.error.flatten())}. ` +
          `Please call extract_arg_graph again with corrected output.`,
      },
    ]
    return extractArgGraph(adapter, retryMessages, system, model, attempt + 1)
  }

  // MAX_RETRIES exhausted — fail-silent, caller returns {}
  console.error('[arg-graph-builder] MAX_RETRIES exhausted — dropping arg graph extraction')
  return null
}
```

When to surface: if 3 consecutive argGraph extractions for the same branch return `null`, emit a Langfuse event `arg_graph_extraction_failure` for monitoring. Do not surface to the user — the Analyst degrades gracefully to an empty argGraph context.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

LangGraph TypeScript node functions are always `async` — the framework `await`s every node's Promise. All existing nodes follow `async function nodeX(state, config): Promise<Partial<GraphState>>`. New Phase 11 nodes must match this signature.

**The one critical mistake in this codebase's context:** the silence-scan `setInterval` callback must be declared `async`, and every `graph.invoke()` inside it must be `await`-ed. If the `await` is dropped, concurrent scan ticks can overlap and the cooldown check becomes a race condition — directly causing Critical Failure Mode 3 (silence trigger re-fires during cooldown).

```typescript
// silence-scan.ts — CORRECT: async callback, awaited graph.invoke
const intervalHandle = setInterval(async () => {
  try {
    await runSilenceScan(supabase, graph)   // await is mandatory — not fire-and-forget
  } catch (err) {
    // Never let an unhandled rejection crash the server process
    console.error('[silence-scan] uncaught error in scan tick', err)
  }
}, SCAN_INTERVAL_MS)

// WRONG — fire-and-forget allows concurrent overlapping ticks:
// setInterval(() => { runSilenceScan(...) }, SCAN_INTERVAL_MS)  // missing await + async
```

Follow the `startAutoFreezeTracker` registration pattern in `auto-freeze.ts` exactly: export a `startSilenceScanLoop(supabase, graph)` function, register it in `server.ts` alongside `startAutoFreezeTracker`.

**Stream vs. await for each node:**

- `FacilitationAgentNode`: stream (forward tokens to `streamWriter` for real-time chat display). On proactive silence-triggered invocations, `streamWriter` is `undefined` — always guard with `config?.configurable?.streamWriter?.(event.text)` (optional chaining, already the existing pattern).
- `AnalyticsAgentNode`: await the full tool use response before returning state. Tool use `input` is only available after the complete `tool_use` event fires — partial streaming is not meaningful here.
- `ArgGraphBuilderNode`: await — must complete and write `argGraph` to state before `AnalyticsAgentNode` reads it. The sequential edge `argGraphBuilder → analysis` enforces this ordering in the graph topology.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt separation:**

Every node passes `system` as a dedicated parameter to `adapter.stream()`, not as a `role: 'system'` entry in the `messages` array. The Anthropic SDK does not use `role: 'system'` in messages — system instructions go in the `system` field of the API request. The existing `adapter.stream(messages, tools, { model, maxTokens, system })` signature correctly separates these. Never embed system instructions in `ProviderMessage[]`.

**Role/Personality prompt composition order (D-03 — locked, non-negotiable):**

```
1. ROLE BEHAVIORAL RULES         ← structurally dominant, always first
2. BLUEPRINT CONTEXT             ← domain name, phase instructions
3. ARGUMENT GRAPH CONTEXT        ← injected structured context (argGraph)
4. PERSONALITY VOICE INSTRUCTIONS ← styling only, always last, never overrides role
```

Never reorder these blocks. If Personality voice appears before Role rules, the model tends to weight later-appearing role rules as corrections to already-established personality tone — increasing the probability of personality bleeding into role behavioral output (Critical Failure Mode 5).

**Few-shot examples:** Include 2-3 BAD/GOOD pairs inline in the Coach and Analyst system prompts. Do not retrieve them dynamically — the behavioral contracts are fixed and the few-shot window is small.

**`maxTokens` — always set explicitly:**

An unset `maxTokens` on any production node causes latency spikes and unexpected cost if the model generates a runaway response. The table in Section 4 lists the per-node caps. Enforce these in code review — treat an unset `maxTokens` in a new node as a blocking issue.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

**Multi-Agent Stateful Workflow strategy for Phase 11:**

The argGraph is the primary compaction mechanism. Instead of passing the full raw conversation history to the Coach and Analyst, `ArgGraphBuilderNode` distills it into a compact `{ nodes, edges }` structure. This makes context injection grow with argument density, not conversation length.

Sliding window for raw messages (defined centrally in a shared constants file):

```typescript
// lib/context-windows.ts — define once, import in all nodes
export const CONTEXT_WINDOWS = {
  facilitation:  10,   // Coach — short-term context for question formulation
  analytics:     20,   // Analyst — needs more context for accurate citation
  argBuild:     100,   // ArgGraphBuilderNode — truncate at 100 messages max
} as const
```

**Conversation summarisation:** Not needed in Phase 11 — the argGraph serves as the conversation summary for AI nodes. If `argGraph.nodes.length > 50` in a later phase, add a compaction step in `ArgGraphBuilderNode` that prunes `confidence < 0.5` nodes and merges duplicate claims before returning the state update. Log `arg_graph_node_count` as a Langfuse span attribute to detect when compaction becomes necessary.

**LangGraph checkpoint compaction:** LangGraph TypeScript 1.4.7 does not include automatic checkpoint compaction (unlike the Python SDK's `create_react_agent` compaction option). For Phase 11, the sliding window approach is sufficient. In a later phase, if checkpoint size grows, add a periodic `graph.updateState(config, { messages: recentMessages })` to truncate the checkpointed message history.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

**Per-call cost estimates at Phase 11 volume:**

Assumptions: 20 active sessions; silence trigger fires at most once per cooldown window (30s default from `bot-arbitrator.ts`); each session generates roughly 1 Coach invocation per 5 minutes at peak activity.

| Invocation | Model tier | Input tokens (est.) | Output tokens (est.) | Cost/call (Haiku-class tier) | Cost/day (20 sessions) |
|------------|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| FacilitationAgentNode (proactive) | fast/light | ~800 (10 msgs + system) | ~80 | ~$0.0001 | ~$0.06 |
| AnalyticsAgentNode (reactive) | analysis | ~1500 (20 msgs + argGraph + system) | ~200 | ~$0.0006 | varies by usage |
| ArgGraphBuilderNode (per-Analyst-invoke) | classification | ~2000 (100 msgs + system) | ~200 | ~$0.0005 | varies by usage |

At 20 sessions, Phase 11 AI cost is negligible (< $0.50/day). The BYOK model means this is the creator's cost surface, not the platform's.

**Caching strategy:**

- `ArgGraphBuilderNode` output is cached in the LangGraph checkpoint (`argGraph` field). The Analyst reads it from state on every invocation — no repeat extraction per graph run.
- Anthropic prompt caching (system prompt cache): Phase 11 system prompts are under 512 tokens — below the 1024-token minimum for Anthropic's prompt caching feature. No action needed. If Blueprint vocabulary causes system prompts to exceed 1024 tokens in future phases, add `cache_control: { type: 'ephemeral' }` to the system block (verify against `@anthropic-ai/sdk` version at that time).
- Exact-match response caching: not appropriate for Coach (responses must vary to avoid repetition) or Analyst (citations must reflect current conversation state).

**Cheaper models for sub-tasks (COST-01 — REQUIREMENTS.md):**

- `OrchestratorNode`: already uses `TASK_MODELS.classification` — cheapest tier.
- `FacilitationAgentNode`: must use `TASK_MODELS.facilitation` (COST-01). Coach moves are short questions — routing them through the `analysis` model wastes ~6x the tokens. If `TASK_MODELS.facilitation` is not yet defined in `model-config.ts`, add it pointing to `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` or equivalent. Verify the model ID against available Anthropic API models before shipping.
- `ArgGraphBuilderNode`: reuse `TASK_MODELS.classification` — structured JSON extraction requires no reasoning beyond what the classification tier provides.

### Dimensions

All dimensions are grounded in Section 1b domain rubric ingredients. The system type is Multi-Agent Stateful Workflow with Proactive Triggers; dimensions cover the multi-agent concerns (inter-agent handoff, goal completion, routing correctness) plus the domain-specific behavioral contracts.

|-----------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------|----------|

### Rubric Notes

**LLM Judge calibration requirement:** The "Genuine inquiry" and "Content-awareness" dimensions use LLM Judge measurement. Before trusting these judges in CI, calibrate each against at least 20 hand-labeled examples from a trained facilitator. Target Pearson correlation ≥ 0.7 between judge scores and human labels. Do not ship automated CI gates based on these dimensions until calibration is complete — use human review in the interim.

**Code-first rule:** All Code-measurable dimensions (trailing `?`, cooldown timestamps, routing log presence, argGraph node count, frozen-session guard) must be implemented as automated assertions in the test suite before any LLM judge is set up. Code checks are deterministic and give immediate feedback during implementation.

| Routing edge cases (silence_gate, analysis_request, no trigger_type) | 3 | Routing correctness: all three paths route to correct node |

**Labeling approach:**

- Code-measurable assertions (trailing `?`, speaker-name regex, routing log presence): automated, no human labeling needed — derive ground truth from test fixtures.
- LLM Judge dimensions (genuine inquiry, content-awareness, role discipline under Personality): initial 20-example calibration set labeled by the session creator acting as domain expert. Compare LLM judge scores against human labels. Accept the judge for CI automation only after Pearson correlation ≥ 0.7 is confirmed.
- Anti-pattern cases (false consensus, role bleed): label manually — these require domain judgment that cannot be fully automated.

**Creation timeline:** Build the 18 examples concurrently with the implementation of `facilitation-agent.ts` and `analytics-agent.ts`, using real model output from each node as it is built. Do not wait until implementation is complete to create the dataset — early examples expose prompt design issues before they become habits.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

These guardrails run on every proactive bot invocation. Each adds latency — keep to the minimum necessary. Three guardrails cover the catastrophic failure modes.

| Guardrail | Trigger condition | Intervention | Implementation |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Frozen session block** | Session `status = 'frozen'` at the time the silence-scan loop evaluates the branch | Block: skip `graph.invoke()` for this branch entirely; log `[silence-scan] skipping frozen session ${branchId}` | Check `session.status` from Supabase query in `runSilenceScan()` before calling `checkSilenceGate()`. No LLM call made. |
| **Cooldown window enforcement** | `triggerMetadata.silence_gate.last_fired_at` is within the cooldown window for the requesting bot (Coach: 5 min default; see PERSONA-03 budget) | Block: skip `graph.invoke()`; log `[silence-scan] cooldown active for ${branchId}, last fired ${last_fired_at}` | Read `last_fired_at` from the PostgresSaver bot-thread checkpoint before invocation. Compare against `Date.now()`. Enforced in `startSilenceScanLoop()` before graph invocation. |
| **Trailing question mark enforcement (Coach)** | `FacilitationAgentNode` produces an `responseText` that does not end with `?` after trimming | Flag: do not insert the malformed message into the `messages` table; emit Langfuse event `coach_contract_violation` with the full `responseText`; log `[facilitation-agent] contract violation: output did not end with ?` | Post-generation check in `facilitationAgentNode()` before the `return` statement. No user sees the violating output. |

**Why no Analyst citation guardrail online:** An Analyst citation failure (no speaker named) is a quality degradation, not a catastrophic failure — the message is still meaningful and does not cause immediate harm. The online guardrail cost (LLM re-check or regex on every Analyst output) is not justified. This failure mode goes to the offline flywheel instead.

### Offline (Flywheel)

These metrics are evaluated on a sampled batch of production interactions. They feed prompt improvement, not real-time intervention.

| Metric | Sampling strategy | Action on degradation |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **Coach question quality rate** — % of Coach outputs rated "genuine inquiry" (not rhetorical closure or statement) by LLM judge | 100% of Coach outputs in first 2 weeks; then random 20% sample after calibration is confirmed | If rate drops below 80% in any 7-day window: review recent Langfuse traces for pattern (Personality drift? argGraph context absent?); adjust system prompt BAD/GOOD examples |
| **Analyst citation presence rate** — % of Analyst outputs containing at least one named speaker | 100% of Analyst outputs sampled via Langfuse; regex check (no LLM cost) | If rate drops below 95%: review argGraph injection — is the speaker field populated in argGraph nodes? Check `ArgNodeSchema.speaker` field population in recent traces. |
| **Silence-break content-awareness score** — LLM judge score (1–5) on whether silence-triggered Coach output references specific conversation content vs. generic prompt | 50% random sample of silence-trigger Coach outputs, scored weekly | If average score drops below 3.5: check whether argGraph is being injected correctly at silence-trigger time (empty argGraph context is the most common root cause); verify `latestArgGraph` fetch in `runSilenceScan()` |
| **argGraph extraction failure rate** — % of ArgGraphBuilderNode invocations that emit `arg_graph_extraction_failure` Langfuse event (null return from `extractArgGraph`) | 100% monitored via Langfuse event stream; no sampling needed — event is already emitted in the code | If rate exceeds 5% on any branch over 10 invocations: check model availability, Zod schema against current ArgNodeSchema definition, and whether the conversation contains parseable claims |
| **Routing correctness** — % of invocations where the router log line (`[graph] START → *`) matches the expected node for the given `trigger_type` | Spot-check 10 invocations per trigger type per week from Langfuse traces | Any routing mismatch triggers immediate investigation — this is a Critical failure mode and should not occur in production if the unit tests pass |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Langfuse (already installed — `@langfuse/langchain@5.9.1`, `@langfuse/otel@5.9.1`, `@langfuse/tracing@5.9.1`). The `CallbackHandler` is already instrumented on every LangGraph execution (OBS-01). Phase 11 nodes are automatically covered — no additional Langfuse setup is required.

Langfuse dashboard: accessible at the configured `LANGFUSE_HOST`. For local dev, check `apps/api/src/server.ts` for the Langfuse client initialization.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

1. **`coach_contract_violation` event count** — fired by the online guardrail when a Coach output does not end with `?`. Target: 0 per day in production. Any non-zero value means the system prompt or Personality injection is breaking the role contract.

2. **`arg_graph_extraction_failure` event count per branch** — fired when `extractArgGraph()` returns `null` after MAX_RETRIES. Target: < 5% of ArgGraphBuilderNode invocations per branch. Sustained failures degrade Analyst citation quality because the argGraph context becomes stale.

3. **`arg_graph_node_count` span attribute** — logged by ArgGraphBuilderNode on every invocation. Track growth per branch over time. Alert if count exceeds 50 (compaction threshold for Phase 12+). Track as a Langfuse score or attribute on the `argGraphBuilder` node span.

4. **`silence_trigger_fire_rate` per branch** — derived from Langfuse traces: count of silence_gate invocations per branch per 15-minute window. Target: ≤ 3 per 15 minutes (PERSONA-03 Coach budget). Sustained overfiring indicates the cooldown enforcement logic is not working correctly.

5. **`facilitation` and `analysis` node p95 latency** — tracked as span duration in Langfuse. Targets: FacilitationAgentNode < 2s p95 (short output, fast model), AnalyticsAgentNode < 5s p95 (longer output, analysis model). Spikes indicate model tier misconfiguration or `maxTokens` not set.

**Alert Thresholds:**

| Condition | Threshold | Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| `coach_contract_violation` count | > 0 in any 24-hour window | Investigate immediately — Coach output is reaching users without a trailing `?`. Check Personality injection order (D-03) in recent traces. |
| `arg_graph_extraction_failure` rate | > 5% on any single branch over 10 consecutive invocations | Investigate Zod schema mismatch or model availability issue. Consider disabling ArgGraphBuilder for that branch until resolved. |
| Silence trigger fires per 15-min window | > 3 for Coach, > 2 for Analyst on any branch | Investigate cooldown enforcement — check `last_fired_at` timestamp reads in `startSilenceScanLoop()`. Likely cause: `await` dropped on `graph.invoke()`, causing concurrent tick overlap. |
| FacilitationAgentNode p95 latency | > 4s | Check `TASK_MODELS.facilitation` model tier — may have been accidentally routed to the analysis model. |
| Bot activity on a frozen session | Any `graph.invoke()` trace where branch session status is frozen | Critical — fix immediately. The frozen-session guard in `runSilenceScan()` is not working. Stop bot activity on that branch manually until fixed. |

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

Rather than reviewing all traces, weight human review toward the interactions most likely to reveal system failures:

1. **All `coach_contract_violation` events** — review 100% of these traces, as each represents a blocked output that the guardrail caught. Look for the specific text pattern that caused the violation.

2. **Silence-trigger Coach outputs where the conversation had an argGraph with 0 nodes** — these are the highest-risk for generic decontextualized responses (the argGraph context was empty, so the Coach had no structured content to reference).

3. **Analyst outputs on branches where `arg_graph_extraction_failure` was emitted in the prior 3 invocations** — the Analyst ran without a valid argGraph context, making citation failures more likely.

4. **Any invocation where `routeFromStart` returned a value that does not appear in the expected routing log** — indicates a routing edge case not covered by tests.

5. **Weekly random 10% sample of silence-trigger Coach outputs** — for LLM judge flywheel scoring (content-awareness dimension). Compare against the 3.5 score threshold defined in Section 6 Offline Flywheel.
