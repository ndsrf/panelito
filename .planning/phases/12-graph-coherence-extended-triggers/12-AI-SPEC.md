## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid — Multi-Agent orchestration extension (new synchronous `TriggerGateNode` detection gate feeding existing Coach/Analyst Role nodes)

**Description:**
`TriggerGateNode` is a detection-only gate, inserted after `ArgGraphBuilderNode`, that evaluates four new Skills belonging to the existing Coach and Analyst personas already live in a synchronous, mobile-first, Spanish-language group chat session (Project Multiverse): semantic-drift redirection (Coach), unlinked-assertion/orphan-edge detection (Analyst), fact-check challenge (Analyst), and tone moderation (Coach). Users are small live groups (debate, coaching, brainstorm sessions) whose session creator BYOKs an AI provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, or Gemini — never hardcoded). "Good" means each Skill fires rarely, cheaply (heuristic/local-embedding tier resolves the overwhelming majority of checks at $0), and — when it does fire — behaves the way a practitioner in the adjacent real-world discipline would: a fact-checker who cites the specific claim and asks for a source rather than asserting a counter-fact; a moderator who nudges gently before escalating; a facilitator who redirects drift as an invitation, not a command; and a dialogue-mapper who never wires a new claim to the wrong (or an unconfirmed) node in the group's shared graph.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Fact-check challenge asserts a confident counter-claim (or fires on a non-checkable opinion/hyperbole) instead of citing the specific message and asking an open question for a source — violates the Analyst's uncertainty-framing discipline (PERSONA-02, D-15) and reads as the bot "winning" an argument rather than facilitating one.
2. Moderation intervention publicly misfires on an innocent or merely emphatic message (heuristic false positive) or escalates straight to a direct/accusatory tone on a first offense instead of a gentle first nudge (D-16) — visibly embarrasses a participant in front of the whole live group with no way to un-send it.
3. Semantic-drift redirect fires on a single tangential remark or legitimate creative digression rather than a sustained 3-message drift trend, reading as the bot policing the conversation instead of facilitating it (D-08).
4. Ghost-edge / orphan-edge fallback wires a new node to the wrong existing node (or to an unconfirmed ghost node) with no visible uncertainty signal, silently corrupting the shared graph the whole group relies on as its consensus record (GRAPH-03, D-13/D-14).
5. Cost-tier routing silently substitutes the wrong `TASK_MODELS` tier (e.g., capable-tier reused for a cheap check, or vice versa) — this produces no type error, only a runtime/eval-level regression, and directly inflates the session creator's own BYOK bill without their knowledge (COST-01/02).

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** General-purpose group deliberation / collaborative decision-making tooling — not a single fixed vertical. Sessions run across multiple Blueprint domains (debate/strategy live today; coaching GROW-model and brainstorming planned) at the discretion of whichever creator hosts the session. Phase 12's four new triggers each borrow discipline from a distinct *adjacent practitioner domain* rather than the product's own vertical: journalism/fact-checking practice (TRIGGER-05), community moderation practice (TRIGGER-06), meeting/workshop facilitation practice (TRIGGER-03), and argumentation/dialogue-mapping practice — IBIS/Kialo-style structured deliberation (TRIGGER-04/GRAPH-03).

**User Population:** Small synchronous groups (roughly 2-12 participants) on mobile devices, primarily Spanish-speaking, in a live, real-time session hosted by a creator who supplies their own AI provider key (BYOK — Anthropic, OpenAI, or Gemini, creator's choice). No professional credentialing is assumed on the human side. The "domain expert" whose judgment matters for evaluation is the discipline being emulated by each Skill (a fact-checker, a moderator, a facilitator, a dialogue-mapper) — not the end participants themselves.

**Stakes Level:** Medium. This is not a medical/legal/financial advice-giving system, so there is no regulatory or physical-harm stakes tier. But the interpersonal stakes are real and immediate: a wrongly-flagged moderation intervention, a condescending fact-check challenge, or a misattributed graph edge is visible to the entire live group at once, cannot be silently retracted (SPEECH-01/02/03 forbid "system artifact" cleanup framing), and can visibly embarrass a participant or damage the group's trust in the tool mid-session.

**Output Consequence:** A firing Skill's output either (a) becomes a node/edge on the shared canvas graph that the Analyst's own future reasoning and the group's eventual synthesis reference directly (GRAPH-04), or (b) becomes a spoken-in-chat intervention that shapes group dynamics for the remainder of the live session. Ghost nodes/edges can be dismissed within 60 seconds (UI-02), but a chat intervention — once delivered into a synchronous multi-user conversation — has no undo for its social effect even if the underlying canvas mutation is later reverted.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Source-citing discipline (fact-check challenge)
Good: Challenge names the specific claim and the message/speaker who made it, then asks an open question for a source ("¿de dónde sacaste ese dato?") — never asserts the claim is false.
Bad: Challenge makes a confident counter-assertion, doesn't reference the specific claim/message it's responding to, or fires on a claim that isn't actually checkable (opinion, rhetorical hyperbole).
Stakes: Critical
Source: Journalism fact-checking practitioner standard (cite every statement of fact to its specific source, verify before publishing) — directly informs D-15/PERSONA-02's uncertainty-framing requirement; corroborated by research showing AI fact-checks read as lower argument quality than human fact-checks when they debunk rather than question (tandfonline.com/doi/10.1080/15213269.2026.2659876).

Dimension: Proportionate, de-escalating moderation tone
Good: First intervention on a participant is a gentle, non-accusatory redirect; tone escalates to a more direct correction only after repeated (N) incidents from the SAME participant in-session (D-16); a single mild message never triggers a strong response.
Bad: First-offense response is already accusatory/direct; escalation tone doesn't track repeat offenses (resets, or is identical every time); a heuristic false positive publicly flags a participant for a message that wasn't actually rude.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Community moderation practitioner consensus — warn before punishing, match consequence to severity, escalate only after repeated violations (bettermode.com/blog/community-moderation, khoros.com/blog/community-moderation) — directly informs D-16's escalating-count design; corroborated by documented false-positive rates in automated moderation systems (arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09175).

Dimension: Light-touch, goal-relative redirection (semantic drift)
Good: Redirect frames itself as an invitation relative to the group's actual topic ("¿cómo se conecta esto con...?"), and fires only on a sustained trailing-window drift trend, not a single off-topic remark.
Bad: Redirect fires after one tangential message, cuts off a legitimate creative digression, or reads as a command to stop rather than a facilitator's question.
Stakes: High
Source: Dialogue-mapping / facilitation practitioner technique (IBIS) — a facilitator redirects by asking whether a comment "adds something new to the map," never by declaring it invalid (argumentree.com/what-is/dialogue-mapping, mindtools.com/aem0r12/dialog-mapping) — directly informs D-08's 3-message trailing window and D-09's per-Blueprint opt-out.

Dimension: Structural connectivity of claims (orphan-edge / unlinked assertion)
Good: Every proposed edge connects the new node to the correct existing COMMITTED node with a correctly-typed Blueprint-vocabulary relationship; the ghost-edge fallback only fires when the LLM itself found no strong connection, and links to the most plausible existing idea.
Bad: An edge is proposed to a semantically unrelated node just to satisfy "must have an edge," or to a ghost (unconfirmed) node — creating a chain of tentative content with no anchor to the group's confirmed reasoning.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Argument/dialogue-mapping practitioner standard (IBIS, Kialo-style structured deliberation) — every claim in a shared map must be traceable to how it relates to prior claims for the map to remain a useful decision record (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue-based_information_system) — directly informs D-13/D-14's committed-only edge-target rule.

Dimension: Cost-tier fidelity (cross-cutting, all four Skills)
Good: The cheap heuristic/local-embedding tier resolves the overwhelming majority of "did this fire" decisions at $0; the capable tier is reached only for confirmed-positive fact-check claims after both tier-1 and tier-2 agree.
Bad: A light-tier model is silently substituted for capable-tier confirmation (or vice versa) — produces no type error, only a runtime/eval-level regression, and directly inflates the session creator's own BYOK bill without their knowledge.
Stakes: High
Source: Project's own COST-01/02 requirements and the BYOK constraint (CLAUDE.md) — the "domain expert" here is the session creator paying for the AI calls; a routing regression is invisible to them until an unexpectedly large invoice.
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **"Well, actually" fact-check tone.** The bot issues a technically-correct but socially tone-deaf correction that reads as a confident debunk rather than an open question, undermining trust in the Analyst persona — research on automated fact-checkers documents this exact gap between human and AI-delivered corrections in perceived respectfulness/argument quality.
2. **Moderation over-triggering on emphatic-but-legitimate Spanish phrasing.** A keyword/tone heuristic pre-filter (D-11) tuned on a curated insult/profanity list plus ALL-CAPS/punctuation signals can false-positive on regional slang or enthusiastic (not abusive) punctuation, publicly flagging an innocent participant with no private-first step — documented false-positive risk in automated chat moderation research scales with message volume.
3. **Drift redirect punishing productive divergence.** Because the domain centroid is a static, Blueprint-level default (D-08) with no session-specific stated goal until Phase 13, a group legitimately exploring an adjacent but unstated sub-topic gets redirected as if off-topic — facilitation practice treats this as a facilitator error, not a participant error.
4. **Ghost-edge miswiring the shared reasoning record.** Because the fallback embeds only `node.label` (D-13) rather than full message context, two differently-argued nodes with superficially similar short labels can be wrongly linked; because the graph is a shared multi-user artifact (not a private per-user output), this error is visible and persistent for the whole group, not just one user.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

None identified as directly binding for this deployment context — no healthcare/financial/legal advice is given, and sessions are creator-hosted private groups rather than an open public platform at scale. Moderation and fact-check triggers touch themes (content moderation, misinformation handling) that carry formal transparency/appeal obligations under some jurisdictions' platform regulation (e.g., EU DSA-style rules) once a service operates at public scale — out of scope today, but worth flagging if the product later opens beyond creator-hosted private sessions.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** LangGraph JS (`@langchain/langgraph` + `@langchain/langgraph-checkpoint-postgres`)

**Version:** `@langchain/langgraph` 1.4.7, `@langchain/langgraph-checkpoint-postgres` 1.0.4 (pinned in `apps/api/package.json`)

**Rationale:**
Already the locked-in framework since Phase 6/7 — Phase 12 is a topology extension (new `TriggerGateNode` + conditional edges into existing `FacilitationAgentNode`/`AnalyticsAgentNode`), not a new build. LangGraph's per-node checkpointing and native Postgres persistence (`PostgresSaver`) are exactly what's needed to add a detection-only gate node between `ArgGraphBuilderNode` and the Role nodes while keeping all bot/trigger state durable across Vercel cold starts (BOT-05). Its conditional-routing primitives (already proven in `orchestrator.ts`'s `DOMAIN_MATCH`/`DOMAIN_BRIDGE`/`DOMAIN_DRIFT` pattern) are the direct template for `TriggerGateNode`'s Skill-based routing.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Plain LangChain chains (greenfield hypothetical) | No native checkpointing — would break the existing `PostgresSaver` thread-state architecture that BOT-04/BOT-05 and Phase 11's silence-scan/arbitration logic already depend on. Not viable mid-project. |
| Any framework swap | Switching frameworks mid-project would break existing Phase 5-11 graph topology, checkpointed thread state, and Langfuse tracing integration. Out of scope by architectural constraint. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — the codebase is already multi-provider. `TASK_MODELS` (`apps/api/src/lib/model-config.ts`) maps every task tier across `anthropic`, `openai`, and `gemini`, and `adapter-factory.ts` constructs the matching provider adapter at runtime (creator-selected, BYOK). CLAUDE.md's "Claude API is the only AI provider in v1" line is stale relative to the current codebase — Phase 12's `TriggerGateNode` and all 4 new Skills must stay provider-agnostic (route through `TASK_MODELS[providerName].*`, never hardcode an Anthropic model ID or Anthropic-specific request shape) so they work identically regardless of which provider a given creator's Blueprint/session is configured for.

### Entry Point Pattern
```typescript
// apps/api/src/graph/nodes/trigger-gate.ts
//
// TriggerGateNode: detection-only. Never generates a bot response (D-06) — it evaluates
// every Role-gated Skill's detect() (D-07: skip a Role's Skills entirely if that Role is
// disabled for the session, exactly like silence-scan.ts:190-191's
// `bot_overrides?.coach ?? bot_defaults?.coach ?? false` pattern) and sets routing metadata
// for the conditional edge to read. Follows the same four-step skeleton as every other node
// in this graph (agent.ts) and the SAME fail-silent contract: never throws.

export async function triggerGateNode(
  state: GraphState,
  // eslint-disable-next-line @typescript-eslint/no-explicit-any
  config?: any,
): Promise<Partial<GraphState>> {
  const blueprint = config?.configurable?.blueprint as Blueprint | undefined
  const sessionBotOverrides = config?.configurable?.botOverrides as Record<string, boolean> | undefined

  if (!blueprint) {
    console.error('[trigger-gate] blueprint missing from config.configurable — no skill fires')
    return { firingSkillId: null, firingSkillRole: null }
  }

  const context: SkillContext = { state, blueprint, config }

  // D-07: Role-activation gate BEFORE evaluating any Skill under that Role.
  const coachEnabled = sessionBotOverrides?.coach ?? blueprint.bot_defaults?.coach ?? false
  const analystEnabled = sessionBotOverrides?.analyst ?? blueprint.bot_defaults?.analyst ?? false

  const candidateSkills: Skill[] = [
    ...(coachEnabled ? COACH_SKILLS : []),
    ...(analystEnabled ? ANALYST_SKILLS : []),
  ]

  // D-05: cheap heuristic tiers run first inside each Skill's own detect() — Promise.all
  // is safe here because each Skill internally gates its own tier-2/tier-3 escalation
  // (COST-02); this only parallelizes the already-gated per-skill checks, it does not
  // bypass the escalation gate (Section 4b.2 — async pitfall).
  const results = await Promise.allSettled(
    candidateSkills.map(async (skill) => ({ skill, result: await skill.detect(context) })),
  )

  // First skill that fires wins (deterministic array order = priority order); a scorer-
  // isolation pattern (bot-arbitrator.ts CR-02) so one throwing Skill never blocks the rest.
  for (const settled of results) {
    if (settled.status === 'rejected') {
      console.warn('[trigger-gate] skill.detect() threw — skipping', settled.reason)
      continue
    }
    const { skill, result } = settled.value
    if (result.fires) {
      console.info('[trigger-gate] skill fired', { id: skill.id, confidence: result.confidence })
      return {
        firingSkillId: skill.id,
        firingSkillRole: skill.role,
        skillMeta: result.meta ?? null,
      }
    }
  }

  return { firingSkillId: null, firingSkillRole: null, skillMeta: null }
}
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It (Phase 12) |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `StateGraph` / `Annotation.Root` | Typed state container with per-field reducers | `GraphState` gains 2-3 new overwrite-style fields (`firingSkillId`, `firingSkillRole`, `skillMeta`) — MUST use the `reducer: (_, v) => v` + `default: () => null` idiom (state.ts convention); a bare `Annotation<T>()` has no default and will throw on first read |
| `addConditionalEdges(source, router, pathsMap)` | Routes from one node to N possible next nodes based on a pure function of state | `TriggerGateNode → facilitation \| analysis \| END`, mirroring `routeAfterOrchestrator`; **every** string the router can return must be a `pathsMap` key or LangGraph throws at compile time |
| `BaseCheckpointSaver` (`PostgresSaver`/`MemorySaver`) | Per-node state snapshot, durable across invocations | No new checkpointer logic needed — `TriggerGateNode`'s output (`firingSkillId` etc.) is just another field on the same checkpointed `GraphState`; survives Vercel cold starts (BOT-05) automatically |
| `graph.getState()` / `graph.updateState()` | Read/write a thread's current checkpoint outside of `.invoke()` | `moderation_count` escalation (D-16) reads/writes via these, same pattern `silence-scan.ts`'s `readCooldownUntil`/`recordCooldown` already use for `triggerMetadata` |
| `Skill` (project-specific, NOT a LangGraph primitive) | `{ id, role, detect(context) → Promise<{fires, confidence, meta}>, buildPromptGuidance(context) → string }` — a plain TS interface | Every one of the 4 new Skills (+ retrofitted `silence-break`) implements this; `TriggerGateNode` is the only LangGraph node that calls `detect()`; `buildPromptGuidance()` is called later, inside `facilitation-agent.ts`/`analytics-agent.ts`'s existing system-prompt builders |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Mixing a fixed `.addEdge()` and `.addConditionalEdges()` from the same source node.** `argGraphBuilder` already has `.addEdge('argGraphBuilder', 'analysis')` (Phase 11). LangGraph does not let a conditional edge "override" a fixed edge from the same source — both fire (fan-out), so `analysis` would run unconditionally on every path in addition to whatever `TriggerGateNode` decides, corrupting the routing. This is the exact same class of bug `graph.ts`'s own comment already documents for `START` ("both cannot coexist; the fixed edge would silently win") — replace, don't add.
2. **`addConditionalEdges` `pathsMap` must list every return value.** Per the Task 1 spike already recorded in `graph.ts` (`graph.test.ts` SPIKE block), an unmapped router return value produces a rejected `graph.invoke()` promise in `@langchain/langgraph@1.4.7`. `routeAfterTriggerGate` returns `'facilitation' | 'analysis' | 'end'` — all three MUST appear as `pathsMap` keys, `'end'` mapped to the imported `END` sentinel.
3. **Overwrite-style `Annotation<T>()` needs an explicit reducer + default.** `state.ts`'s own comment: LangGraph's no-arg `Annotation<T>()` creates a LastValue channel but does NOT support a default value. The 2-3 new fields this phase adds (`firingSkillId`, `firingSkillRole`, `skillMeta`) must follow the existing `reducer: (_, v) => v, default: () => null` idiom already used for `guardrailResult`/`agentOutput`/`driftAction` — a bare `Annotation<T>()` throws `undefined` on the first read inside `routeAfterTriggerGate` before any node has set it.
4. **Loading the ONNX pipeline per-invocation instead of as a module singleton.** `@huggingface/transformers`'s own docs (`tutorials/node.md`) recommend a singleton class with `static instance = null` — re-calling `pipeline('feature-extraction', ...)` inside `detect()` on every graph invocation reloads and re-initializes the ~90MB `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` model from disk/cache on every message, adding hundreds of ms to every drift-redirect and orphan-edge check. Cache the **promise**, not just the resolved value (`let _extractorPromise: Promise<FeatureExtractionPipeline> | null = null`), so concurrent `detect()` calls from `Promise.allSettled` in `TriggerGateNode` await the same in-flight load instead of racing two separate `pipeline()` calls.
5. **A throwing `Skill.detect()` must not take down `TriggerGateNode`.** Unlike `bot-arbitrator.ts`'s `runArbitration()` (which already isolates scorer exceptions per bot — CR-02 precedent), a naive `for (const skill of skills) await skill.detect()` loop without a try/catch per iteration lets one buggy Skill (e.g. a malformed regex in the moderation heuristic) silently prevent `fact-check`/`orphan-edge` from ever being evaluated on that turn. Use `Promise.allSettled`, not `Promise.all`, and treat `'rejected'` as "this skill did not fire," never as a reason to abort the whole node.
6. **`config.configurable` seam divergence between the node and the Skill.** Every existing node (`orchestrator.ts`, `facilitation-agent.ts`) reads adapters/blueprint directly off `config.configurable`. Because `Skill.detect(context)` is a plain function, not a graph node, it must receive an equivalent `SkillContext` object explicitly constructed by `TriggerGateNode` — do not have individual Skill modules reach back into a global/module-level config; this breaks the test-injection seam pattern (`agentAdapter`/`classifierAdapter`/`facilitationAdapter`) this codebase relies on for `vitest` unit tests.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice. This project uses **Zod**, not Pydantic (TypeScript/Node.js stack) — the heading below is kept per template convention but the content is Zod/TypeScript throughout.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

This project's structured-output pattern is **Zod schema + `.safeParse()` + a bounded retry loop with a correction message appended to the conversation** — the exact pattern `arg-graph-builder.ts`'s `attemptExtraction()` already implements. Phase 12 reuses this pattern for two new structured outputs:

```typescript
// packages/types/src/skill.ts — Skill.detect()'s return shape. Zod-validated at the
// TriggerGateNode boundary even though detect() is a plain function, not an LLM call for
// most Skills — this keeps the contract identical whether a Skill's detect() is 100%
// heuristic (moderation) or ends in an LLM tool-use call (fact-check tier-2/3).
import { z } from 'zod'

export const SkillDetectionResultSchema = z.object({
  fires: z.boolean(),
  confidence: z.number().min(0).max(1),
  /** Skill-specific payload the firing Role node's buildPromptGuidance() may need —
   *  e.g. fact-check's flagged claim text, moderation's escalation tier, orphan-edge's
   *  ghost-fallback target node id. Untyped at this layer (z.unknown per key) because each
   *  Skill defines its own meta shape; individual Skill modules should narrow this with
   *  their own local Zod schema before reading fields off it. */
  meta: z.record(z.string(), z.unknown()).optional(),
})

export type SkillDetectionResult = z.infer<typeof SkillDetectionResultSchema>
```

Retry logic summary: **1 retry** (not 2) for the tier-2/tier-3 fact-check classifiers — they are single-field-ish tool calls, not the 4-field-per-node/edge extraction `arg-graph-builder.ts` handles. Log every validation failure with a `[fact-check]`-prefixed `console.error` including `parsed.error.flatten()` (matches every other node's logging convention in this codebase). Surface to the user: never directly — a failed classification silently resolves to `fires: false` (fail-closed), consistent with every other node's fail-silent contract (`TriggerGateNode` itself also never throws).

### Async-First Design

LangGraph JS nodes are plain `async` functions returning `Promise<Partial<GraphState>>` — there is no separate sync/async API split to get wrong (unlike Python's `asyncio.run()`-in-a-running-loop trap). The Phase 12–specific async pitfalls are:

1. **`Promise.all` vs `Promise.allSettled` when evaluating multiple Skills.** Use `Promise.allSettled` (Common Pitfall 5 above) — a single rejected Skill must not abort evaluation of the other 3-5 candidate Skills in the same `TriggerGateNode` invocation.
2. **The ONNX pipeline singleton must cache the in-flight Promise, not just the resolved pipeline.** Two concurrent `detect()` calls (e.g. `drift-redirect` and `orphan-edge` both wanting the same embedding model within the same `Promise.allSettled` batch) racing against a `null`-checked-then-assigned singleton will both call `pipeline(...)` before either resolves — `if (this.instance === null) { this.instance = pipeline(...) }` is NOT safe under concurrent callers in this exact pattern *unless* the assignment happens synchronously before the first `await` (which the Transformers.js docs' own singleton example does correctly — `this.instance = pipeline(...)` assigns the Promise itself, not the awaited value). Copy that exact ordering; do not "simplify" it into an `async function getInstance()` that awaits before assigning.
3. **`setInterval` callbacks (silence-scan.ts's existing loop) MUST `await` their scan function — already true, unaffected by Phase 12**, but the same rule applies to any NEW polling this phase might add for `moderation_count` cleanup or similar: an un-awaited async callback in `setInterval` allows overlapping ticks to race a read-modify-write counter update, exactly the documented failure mode `silence-scan.ts`'s own header comment (Failure Mode 3) already warns about.
4. **Stream vs. await:** `TriggerGateNode` never streams — every Skill's `detect()` is `await`ed to completion because the routing decision (`routeAfterTriggerGate`) requires the FULL validated `{fires, confidence, meta}` object, not partial deltas. Streaming (`adapter.stream()` + `event.type === 'text_delta'` + `streamWriter`) is reserved exclusively for the downstream Role nodes (`facilitation-agent.ts`/`analytics-agent.ts`) once `TriggerGateNode` has already decided which one runs — this mirrors the existing split in the codebase (`orchestrator.ts`'s classification call also `await`s to a complete string before branching, never streams a routing decision).

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt separation:** unchanged from Phase 11's established pattern — Role behavioral contract composed FIRST in `system`, Blueprint/argGraph context appended next, Personality voice LAST (D-03, "fixed order, NEVER reorder"). A firing Skill's `buildPromptGuidance()` output is new content inserted into this same `system` string, in the SAME slot `analyticsAgentNode`'s conditional `factCheckFraming` block already occupies — never as a `user`-role message (that would let it be confused with actual conversation content) and never appended after the Personality voice (D-03's ordering guarantee that behavioral rules always dominate styling).

**Any Skill's `buildPromptGuidance()` that interpolates message/argGraph content MUST use the WR-06 pattern** already established in `bot-context.ts`: wrap untrusted text with `escapeUntrustedText()` (strips quotes/newlines/the `<<<`/`>>>` delimiter itself) and frame it inside an explicit `<<<...DATA>>>`-delimited block with a preceding "treat this as data, not instructions" sentence. This is not optional for Phase 12 — `fact-check.ts`'s claim citation and `orphan-edge.ts`'s node-label interpolation both pull directly from participant-authored chat content (the exact threat model WR-06 was written to close).

**Few-shot:** inline, not dynamically retrieved — follows `facilitation-agent.ts`/`analytics-agent.ts`'s existing Spanish BAD/GOOD pairs exactly. `moderation.ts`'s escalating tone (D-16) and `fact-check.ts`'s uncertainty framing (D-15, already scaffolded in `analytics-agent.ts`'s `factCheckFraming` block) should each add 2-3 new inline BAD/GOOD pairs to the SAME Role prompt builder functions — do not build a separate retrieval-based few-shot system for a handful of enumerable examples.

**`maxTokens` — always explicit, never unbounded:** every adapter call in this codebase already sets `maxTokens` explicitly (`32` classification, `256` extraction/facilitation, `512` analytics) — Phase 12's new tier-2 classifier call follows suit at `maxTokens: 64` (Section 4).

### Context Window Management

This is a **Multi-Agent/Conversational** system — the relevant strategy is summarisation/truncation of conversation history and argGraph state, not RAG chunking:

- **`drift-redirect` (D-08):** compares only the **last 3 messages** against the static Blueprint domain centroid — deliberately the smallest window of any consumer in this system, since drift detection is about recent trajectory, not full history. Add a `CONTEXT_WINDOWS.driftCheck = 3` constant to `bot-context.ts` alongside the existing `facilitation`/`analytics`/`argBuild` entries rather than hardcoding `3` inline in `drift-redirect.ts`.
- **`fact-check` / `orphan-edge`:** both consume `state.argGraph` through the SAME shared `summarizeArgGraph()` path the Analyst already uses (GRAPH-04, D-14's "single shared context path" rule) — do not have either Skill build its own argGraph summary. Note the existing risk (unchanged by Phase 12, but now exercised by two more callers): `summarizeArgGraph()` currently dumps the FULL node/edge list with no truncation — as `state.argGraph` grows across a long session this is an unbounded prompt-size risk shared by every consumer, not something Phase 12 introduces or is scoped to fix; flag it to `gsd-planner` if session length in practice exceeds ~150-200 argGraph nodes.
- **`moderation`:** needs only the single triggering message plus the `moderation_count` counter (D-16) — no conversation-history window at all. This is the cheapest-context Skill of the four.
- **`silence-break` (retrofitted, D-03):** unchanged — still uses `CONTEXT_WINDOWS.facilitation` (10 messages) via the existing `fetchRecentMessages()` path in `silence-scan.ts`.

### Cost and Latency Budget

**Per-call cost estimate at expected volume** (Anthropic pricing, illustrative — verify current rates):
- Tier-1 (heuristic + local ONNX embedding): **$0** per check, applies to every message on every active branch with Coach/Analyst enabled — this is the volume workhorse and MUST stay LLM-free (COST-02's entire premise).
- Tier-2 (`claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, ~64 output tokens + short system prompt): sub-cent per call; only reached when tier-1's heuristic pattern-match already flagged the message (D-12) — expected to fire on a small minority of messages.
- Tier-3 (`claude-sonnet-4-6`, reuses existing `analyticsAgentNode` 512-token call): the most expensive tier, gated behind BOTH tier-1 AND tier-2 confirming — by design this should fire rarely relative to total message volume, which is the entire point of the 3-tier escalation gate (COST-02).

**Caching:** the Blueprint domain centroid embedding (D-08) is computed **once per Blueprint load** and cached in memory — never re-embedded per message. The ghost-edge fallback embeds only `node.label` (D-13, not full message content) and only runs when the LLM's own edge proposal already failed, keeping it a rare, cheap path rather than a per-message cost. Neither needs exact-match or semantic response caching (no repeated identical LLM calls to cache) — the cost lever here is entirely the escalation-tier gate, not a cache layer.

**Sub-task model routing (COST-01/02 validation):** this phase is explicitly a validation exercise for the EXISTING `TASK_MODELS` registry, not a reason to add new tiers — confirm via test that `fact-check.ts`'s tier-2 call always resolves to `TASK_MODELS[providerName].classification` and tier-3 always resolves to `TASK_MODELS[providerName].analysis`, never the reverse (a light-tier model silently reused for the capable-tier fact-check confirmation would be a silent regression of COST-02's whole purpose, and would not raise a type error — only a runtime/eval-level check catches it).

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** 20 examples minimum (Critical-priority dimensions across all 4 Skills — the production tier, not the 10-example starting minimum).

**Composition** (≈5 examples per Skill):
- **Fact-check (5):** checkable claims (numbers, dates, absolute qualifiers) that SHOULD fire tier-2/3; non-checkable opinion/hyperbole that should be filtered at tier-1; a confirmed-positive claim to validate the citation+question response shape (D-15).
- **Moderation (5):** genuinely rude/disruptive messages; innocent emphatic/regional-slang messages (false-positive candidates for D-11's keyword list); a same-participant repeat-offense sequence to validate D-16's tone escalation.
- **Drift-redirect (5):** a sustained 3-message drift trend that SHOULD fire; a single tangential remark that should NOT fire; a legitimate creative digression that should NOT be redirected; a Blueprint with `drift_detection_enabled=false` that never fires regardless of content.
- **Orphan-edge / ghost-edge (5):** a new node with an obvious semantic match (LLM should propose the edge directly, no fallback needed); an ambiguous node requiring the ghost-edge cosine-sim fallback; a node with a superficially similar but semantically wrong candidate target (tests D-13's known miswiring failure mode); a scenario with only ghost (uncommitted) candidate nodes present, verifying D-14's committed-only rule holds.

**Labeling:**
- Native Spanish-speaking pilot participants label heuristic false positives/negatives for the moderation keyword list (D-11) and fact-check claim-pattern regex (D-12) — closest available proxy to a professional moderator/fact-checker.
- Session creator / product owner (domain-expert proxy) calibrates drift cosine threshold and moderation escalation count (N) from this dataset plus Langfuse trace review.
- Facilitation-literate reviewer (informal, IBIS heuristic) labels drift-redirect and ghost-edge cases during Verify phase.
- LLM judge (Promptfoo `llm-rubric`) scores fact-check/moderation/drift response tone against Dimensions 1-3's PASS text — **must be calibrated to ≥0.7 correlation with the human labels above before any judge score is trusted in CI gating** (per `ai-evals.md`'s calibration requirement).

**Creation Timeline:** starts alongside Task 1 (Skill scaffolding), not batched at the end — each Skill's implementation PR adds its own ~5 reference examples before merge, so the dataset and the code mature together.

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Langfuse (already integrated — `@langfuse/langchain` `CallbackHandler` + `@langfuse/otel` span processor, per-request instantiation per OBS-01/02; module-level singleton explicitly forbidden per REQUIREMENTS.md's Out of Scope table). Phase 12 tags every `TriggerGateNode` span with `firingSkillId`, `tierReached`, and `providerName` — extending the existing tracing coverage ahead of COST-03's full cost-attribution dashboard (Phase 14).

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Skill fire rate per Skill per session** (moderation, fact-check, drift-redirect, orphan-edge) — volume health check; each Skill should fire rarely relative to message count, per Section 1b's "good = fires rarely" framing.
2. **Fact-check tier distribution** — % of firings resolved at tier-1 heuristic vs. tier-2 classifier vs. tier-3 capable — direct measure of COST-02's escalation-gate efficacy.
3. **Moderation escalation-count distribution** — how many participants reach the tier-2/tier-3 tone tier in a session — measures D-16's de-escalation design working as intended (should be a small minority).
4. **Ghost-edge fallback rate** — % of edge proposals that hit the D-13 fallback vs. the LLM proposing directly — graph coherence health signal for GRAPH-03.
5. **`TriggerGateNode` p50/p95 latency** — this node now gates every message before the Role nodes; ONNX singleton misconfiguration (Pitfall 4) or `Promise.allSettled` fan-out cost would show up here first.
6. **Per-provider routing correctness** — % of tier-2/tier-3 calls whose resolved model ID matches the `TASK_MODELS[providerName]` expectation, tagged by `providerName` — catches a COST-01/02 regression live, not just in CI.

**Alert Thresholds:**
- `TriggerGateNode` p95 latency > 800ms → investigate (likely ONNX singleton not caching the in-flight promise, Pitfall 4 regression).
- Fact-check tier-3 fire rate > 5% of tier-1-flagged messages, sustained over a session → tier-1/tier-2 pattern-match likely too permissive; cost risk to the creator's BYOK bill.
- Moderation false-positive rate (from the weekly human-labeled sample) > 10% → recalibrate D-11 keyword list.
- Any single participant's `moderation_count` exceeds 5 within 15 minutes in one session → escalate to the session creator directly (possible genuine bad-actor case beyond a de-escalation Skill's own scope).
- The cost-tier resolution guardrail (Section 6) fires even once in production → page immediately; this is a silent billing regression, Critical per Section 1b's cost-tier-fidelity dimension.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- **100%** human review of moderation and fact-check firings — rarest and highest interpersonal-stakes Skills; feeds the offline flywheel's false-positive-rate and tone-score metrics directly.
- **20% weighted-random** sample of drift-redirect and orphan-edge firings — higher expected volume, lower per-instance stakes than moderation/fact-check.
- **100%** of sessions where the cost-tier guardrail fired, or where any Skill hit its second-tier retry-and-fail-closed path — these are exactly the sessions most likely to reveal a routing or escalation-gate regression.
- Bias sampling toward: sessions on non-Anthropic providers (`openai`/`gemini`) to catch provider-specific drift that anthropic-only manual testing would miss; multi-firing sessions (same Skill firing >2×) as likely threshold-miscalibration signals.
